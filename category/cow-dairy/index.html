Raw Cow Milk

from $5.50

RawA2/A2100% grass-fed

Whole milk, straight from the herd. The percentages are butterfat: 6.3% is all-Jersey, 4.8% is a mixed European herd (Swiss, Dutch Belted, Shorthorn), and the Guernsey is very creamy, high in protein and golden from beta-carotene. Guernsey is limited, so Jersey stands in when it runs out.

We carry raw cow milk from three kinds of herds. All three are 100% grass-fed whole milk, straight from the cow. The difference is in the breed, and you can taste it.

  • Jersey, 6.3% butterfat: very rich and creamy, A2/A2.
  • Guernsey: golden from beta-carotene, high in protein and fat, A2/A2.
  • European herd, 4.8%: a mixed herd of Swiss, Dutch Belted and Shorthorn cows, a little lighter.

The percentages are butterfat. Every one of them is whole milk: nothing is skimmed off or added.

About the Guernsey

Kathy saw how rare Guernsey milk is and started her own Guernsey herd to bring it to members. It's the only organic Guernsey farm we know of on the East Coast, apart from one in Vermont. The herd is small, so when Guernsey runs out, we substitute Jersey.

Keeping it fresh

Get it into the fridge fast and keep it at the back, where it's coldest. Glass keeps milk colder than plastic. The cream rises to the top, so shake the bottle before pouring, or skim the cream off for your coffee.

Raw Heavy Cream

from $6.00

RawA2/A2

Thick enough to stand a spoon in. Whip it into icing, churn it into about 1¼ lb of butter per quart, or float a spoonful of whipped cream on your coffee. Thickness changes with the seasons. Keep it longest by moving it to glass and setting it in a bowl of ice water in the fridge.

Our heaviest cream, from raw, grass-fed Jersey and Guernsey milk. It's thick enough to stick to your spoon, and its thickness changes with the seasons and the pasture.

Ways to use it

  • Whip it for buttercream icing
  • Float a spoonful of whipped cream on your coffee, like a cappuccino
  • Churn it into butter at home: a quart of heavy cream makes about 1¼ lb (light cream makes about 1 lb)
  • Make ice cream

How to care for it once it's home

Move it into a glass jar right away. Set the jar in a bowl of ice water in the fridge, and top up the ice as it melts. Sitting in near-freezing water, the cream keeps longer and won't sour as quickly. You can do the same with salted and unsalted butter.

Raw Cow Kefir

from $8.00

RawA2/A2

An old way to keep milk. Kefir grains eat up the milk's natural sugars, and it keeps for months.

Kefir goes back thousands of years. Travelers used to ferment milk in skin bags as they crossed long distances. Ours is cultured with kefir grains, which feed on the natural sugars in the milk, so the finished kefir is tangy and only lightly sweet.

Kept cold, kefir lasts a long time: it keeps culturing slowly and simply gets more tart.
