Beef Bulgogi

Sold out

Grass-fedSold out

Thin slices of beef in a sweet-savory Korean marinade. It cooks in minutes.

Bulgogi means "fire meat": thin slices of beef soaked in a sweet, savory marinade, then seared fast over high heat until the edges catch and caramelize. Our version starts with 100% grass-fed ribeye, which is lean and full of flavor, so it soaks up the marinade beautifully.

Why it works

  • Slice it thin: the beef cooks in seconds and stays tender
  • The grated pear tenderizes the meat and adds natural sweetness
  • A very hot pan gives you those smoky, charred edges

The recipe (serves 4 to 6, all organic)

  • 1½ lb grass-fed boneless ribeye
  • ½ small pear, peeled and grated (an apple works too)
  • ¼ cup soy sauce, or coconut aminos
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar, or raw honey
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tbsp gochujang (Korean red chili paste), to taste
  • 2 green onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
  • 2 tbsp grass-fed ghee or tallow, for the pan

How to make it

  • Firm up the steak in the freezer for 20 to 30 minutes, then slice it as thin as you can, across the grain.
  • Whisk the pear, soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic, ginger and gochujang. Toss the beef in it, cover and refrigerate for 2 hours or overnight.
  • Heat a cast-iron skillet or grill pan until it's smoking hot and add a little ghee.
  • Cook the beef in small batches, 1 to 2 minutes a side, so it sears instead of steaming.
  • Finish with green onions and sesame seeds.

Serve it with

  • Steamed rice
  • Kimchi from our fermented shelf
  • Lettuce or perilla leaves for wrapping
  • Sliced cucumbers
